Reuse that pays every month.

The world is learning to resell old devices. Nobody runs them afterwards. amader.cloud takes working devices that people and companies no longer use, gives each one a job, and charges every month to keep it running. We're starting with the apartment buildings of Dhaka.

The gap

Today, a working device that someone stops using has three possible futures. A repair shop resells it once. A recycler shreds it. Or it sits in a drawer, still plugged in and never updated, until it becomes part of a botnet.

None of these keeps the device working for anyone. That's the business we're building: we operate reused devices as a service.

The model

  • Paid at both ends. Companies pay per device to retire them. Homes, buildings, shops and schools pay monthly to run them.
  • Hardware close to free. Our supply is donated or paid for by the company retiring it.
  • Recurring revenue. Every installed device sits under a monthly plan.
  • The building is the customer. Pooled devices fix shared problems: backups held by neighbours, lobby screens that replace posters and sell local ads, gate logs, alerts. One committee decision, many paying flats.
  • Public sector. Fixed-fee reviews that size government digital projects to the real need, then reconditioned devices processed to government standards. The Procurement Rules 2025 now name sustainable procurement and asset disposal.
  • A marketplace on top. Members rent out idle storage and computing to researchers and startups. We keep a share, and members' net cost falls.
  • Network effect. Each new member adds storage and backup locations that make the service better for everyone else.

Why Dhaka first

One sale, many customers

Dhaka lives in apartment buildings. One committee decision brings in cameras, Wi‑Fi and a building server, and then flats join one by one.

Subscriptions already work

bKash and Nagad make small monthly payments normal, with no credit card needed.

The rules just changed

The Personal Data Protection Act 2026 makes keeping data local and wiping devices properly a legal question. That's exactly what we sell.

Early unit economics

These are our working assumptions. The pilot will test them.

  • Tk 4,490monthly revenue per building

    A 30-flat building where 10 flats join. About Tk 2,600 a month is left after support, visits and network costs.

  • 2×retirement fee vs cost

    About Tk 500 per laptop against roughly Tk 250 for wiping, testing and logistics. The device then goes into a monthly plan.

What we'll know that nobody else does

Every device we handle carries a passport: what it is, how it was wiped, what job it does, how long it lasts, what it costs to run. Over time, this becomes the most detailed record anywhere of which second-hand devices are worth reusing, and for what. That's how we'll price, choose devices and expand faster than anyone starting after us.
